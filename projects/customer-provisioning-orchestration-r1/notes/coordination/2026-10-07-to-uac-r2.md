## A. ACTION — demo self-registration SPE grant markers (your task-171 code) — issue #1363

Our adversarial review of the 2026-10-07 master merge found three problems in how demo self-registration records and
removes its SPE grant. They are **latent on dev today**, because registry record "Demo 1" has no `sprk_specontainerid`, so
Step 8 grants nothing. They become live once a demo container is configured.

1. **Marker keyed to the wrong Dataverse.**
   - `DemoProvisioningService` Step 6 creates the systemuser in the demo environment's Dataverse (`environment.DataverseUrl`, `:146`).
   - Step 8 records the grant under `SprkStd{that systemuserid}` (`GrantMarkedWriterAsync`, `:257-261`).
   - `SpeContainerMembershipSync` reads business units and users from the BFF's **own** Dataverse (`:95`, `:116`).
   - On dev those differ: the BFF's own Dataverse is `spaarkedev1`; "Demo 1" points at `spaarke-demo`.
   - If the demo container is also a business-unit container the sync sees, that holder can never be read. The marker is kept and counted *unknown* on every run, forever.
2. **Expiry removes the grant but not the marker.** `DemoExpirationService.RevokeSpeContainerAccessAsync` (`:308-350`) deletes the permission directly instead of calling `RemoveMarkedGrantAsync`, so the `SprkStd…` marker stays. When the BFF's Dataverse and the demo's are the same, the standing pass then sees a stale marker. Its rule is "clear it and grant again if the holder is eligible", so an expired demo user whose systemuser is still enabled in that unit would get the writer role back.
3. **The expiry list and delete break the marked-grant rules.** Only the first page of permissions is read, and the delete has no `Prefer: onlyRemoveContainerScopedPermission`. `RemoveMarkedGrantAsync` does both correctly.

Suggested fix (yours to validate):
- decide whose identity a demo grant is recorded against: a marker the sync can resolve, or a separate demo prefix that the standing pass ignores;
- expire through `RemoveMarkedGrantAsync`, with an access read from `ReadAccessAsync`;
- add a provision → expire → standing-pass test: no re-grant, no orphan marker.

Since our T227d (see E), Step 8 and expiry also only work on a container the hosting BFF owns: one listed in
`SharePointEmbedded__OwnedContainerIds` or carrying the stamp's `spaarkeCustomerId` marker. Otherwise Step 8 is skipped
with a warning. Configuring a demo container needs that step too. That part is ours; tell us when you configure one.

## E. FYI — how your task 171 was merged with our T227d ownership guard (PR #1365)

**The binding rule:** every app-only SharePoint Embedded Graph client comes from `SpeContainerOwnershipGuard.ForOwnedContainerAsync(containerOrDriveId, ct)`, never `IGraphClientFactory.ForApp()`. Each Model 1 stamp's identity holds application `full` on the shared container type, so an unguarded app-only call reaches every customer's containers. The guard refuses any container the stamp neither configured nor created, with a 404 `spe_container_not_owned`. ArchTest `SpeAppOnlyContainerGuardTests` fails the build on any other `.ForApp()` caller.

What changed in your code when we merged #1333:
- **`DriveItemOperations` / `UploadSessionManager`:** your app-only twins (DownloadFile, GetFileMetadataUncached, DownloadFileVersion, GetCurrentVersionId, GetEmbedPreviewUrl, CreateSharingLink, GetDriveItem, UploadSmall, ReplaceFileContent, CreateUploadSession) get their client from the guard before the shared `*CoreAsync`. The OBO twins are unchanged.
- **`SpeContainerMembershipService`:** ReadAccess, ReadMarkers, GrantMarkedWriter and RemoveMarkedGrant use the guard. A refused container **throws** the 404 before any Graph call, so `GrantMarkedWriterAsync` / `RemoveMarkedGrantAsync` no longer return `Failed` for it. The constructor takes the guard (`SpeContainerOwnershipGuard`, logger).
- **`SpeContainerMembershipSync`:** a refused container is a **skip** in the JIT pass (no JIT grant can exist there) and a **named failure** in the standing pass (a business unit stamped with a container this stamp doesn't own).
- **`OfficeEditAccessService`:** a refused container surfaces as the 404 (in practice `GetDriveItemAsync` refuses first).
- **`DemoProvisioningService`:** builds its membership service over the guard.
- **Revoke/closure:** `RevokeMembershipAsync` / `RemoveMembershipsAsync` still report a refused container as a failed result rather than throwing. That is pinned in `SpeAppOnlyContainerIsolationTests`.
- **Your tests:**
  - `SpeContainerMembershipSyncTests` and `OfficeEditAccessServiceTests` now mock with `TestSpeOwnership.AllowAll(factory)`;
  - `SpeBrokerOnlyByteIdentityGuardTests` no longer lists the workspace pre-fill services (our T227f retired their staging upload; pre-fill extracts in memory);
  - `ActivityCurrentDisposalGuardTests`' floor is 14 (T227d removed the app-only `ContainerOperations.ListContainersAsync`);
  - `SpeAppOnlyContainerIsolationTests` now covers your 13 app-only paths.

**Going forward:** a new app-only SPE call takes its client from the guard; tests build it with `TestSpeOwnership.AllowAll(...)` (permissive) or `TestSpeOwnership.Real(...)`. When you merge master after #1365, grep for `.ForApp(` and run `SpeAppOnlyContainerGuardTests`.
