# State of Westmark Office of the Attorney General: Special Counsel Billing Requirements

**State of Westmark, Office of the Attorney General** · Version FY2026 · Effective 2025-07-01

> Synthetic document written for Spaarke's billing-guideline evaluation. The organisation is fictional.

The Attorney General appoints special counsel under state law to represent state agencies. These requirements are part of every appointment; where an appointment letter sets a different term, the letter controls.

## 1. Appointment

**1.1** Special counsel may not represent any party adverse to the State or a state agency while the appointment lasts.

**1.2** Invoices may be public records. Descriptions must show the work done without disclosing privileged strategy.

## 2. Personnel

**2.1** The appointment letter lists the attorneys and staff approved to work on the matter. Only those personnel may bill it. An invoice that includes time by anyone else is returned unpaid.

## 3. Rates

**3.1** Hourly rates may not exceed the maximum for the timekeeper's classification in Appendix A. Time billed at a higher rate is reduced to the Appendix A maximum.

**3.2** Rates charged to the State are no higher than the lowest rates the firm charges any other public client.

## 4. Recording time

**4.1** Time is recorded in increments of one-tenth of an hour (0.1 hour). An entry in any other increment is reduced to the next lower tenth.

**4.2** No attorney or staff member may bill more than 10 hours in one day. Time above 10 hours in a day is reduced to 10 hours.

**4.3** Each entry describes the specific task performed. Vague descriptions such as "attention to matter" or "review file" are referred for review.

## 5. Expenses

**5.1** Word processing (UTBMS expense code E103), facsimile (E104) and online legal research (E106) are overhead and are disallowed.

**5.2** Photocopying (E101) is reimbursed at no more than $0.15 per page, and delivery and messenger services (E107) at no more than $40.00 per delivery. Amounts above those limits are reduced to the limit.

**5.3** Travel outside the State needs the Attorney General's approval in advance.

## 6. Invoices

**6.1** Invoices are submitted monthly, and the Office must receive each within 30 days after the end of the month billed. A later invoice is referred for review.

**6.2** Every time entry carries a UTBMS litigation task code and an activity code. An invoice with a time entry that lacks its codes is returned unpaid.

**6.3** Work must fall within the matter's open and close dates; entries dated outside them are referred for review. An invoice whose total exceeds the budget approved for the matter is referred for review.

**6.4** An entry that repeats another entry for the same timekeeper, date, hours and description is a duplicate and is disallowed.

**6.5** The final invoice is submitted within 60 days after the matter closes.

**6.6** When an entry or invoice does not comply with these guidelines, the outcome is the one the clause states: the amount is reduced to what the clause allows; the charge is disallowed and not paid; the item is referred for review, which may lead to a question to the firm or an adjustment; or the whole invoice is returned unpaid for correction and resubmission.

**6.7** No interest or late fee accrues on any amount the State owes.
