## 4. "Cascade" names two different mechanisms — this nearly cost us both

Your commit `63f6b3c4c` says *"cascade cannot express the access rule"*. Our plan says the parent→child
cascade is the core of the model. **Both are correct, because "cascade" is being used for two different
things.**

| | Mechanism | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **Yours** | **Dataverse-native Parental relationship cascade** (`Reparent + Share = Cascade All`) | ❌ Correctly rejected |
| **Ours** | **BFF-computed inheritance** — term 5 of the evaluator; the child is authorized by evaluating rights on its core ancestor | ✅ Still the model |

Your rejection reasoning is right and we are adopting it as recorded fact: **access reaching the parent via
security-role or privilege-depth scope never inherits to children** — and that is the normal case. Native
cascade delivers only for owner, owning-team, and explicitly-shared principals. Plus Dataverse permits one
parental relationship per child table, so `sprk_matter` and `sprk_project` cannot both be parental.

**Your replacement — "on a document-level denial, evaluate the same right on the parent and allow on
success" — IS our term 5, implemented at the filter instead of in the evaluator.** We converged. Please
adopt the vocabulary split above in your plan so the next reader does not re-derive the contradiction:
say **"Parental cascade"** for the Dataverse feature and **"inherited term" / "parent-fallback"** for the
computed one.

## 5. Parent→child access — we own it, here is the actual model

**Ownership: `unified-access-control-r2`, Phase 3 (tasks 050–058), FR-26 + FR-27.** Our project CLAUDE.md
states the parent→child cascade "seeded the project." Your `243b0f864` guessed we might be the owner —
confirmed, we are.

### The authoritative evaluator (design.md §4, verbatim)

```
ADDITIVE  (union; HIGHEST WINS)
  1. Dataverse answer      Type 1 ONLY
  2. Explicit grant        sprk_externalrecordaccess (contact or org), carries level
  3. Derived member        allow-listed sprk_assigned* lookups → contact + org identities
  4. Org expansion         org identity → active contacts via sprk_contactorganization
  5. Inheritance           child takes its core ancestor's rights

VETO  (after the max; order matters)
  6. Deny list             ethical wall + per-child revocation   → None
  7. Restricted            sprk_accesspermission = Restricted    → None for ALL contacts
  8. Secure                sprk_issecure = true → suppress terms 3 + 4 BEFORE the max
```

`"No Access"` is a **veto, never a level** — under highest-wins `max()` would ignore it and an ethical wall
would fail silently in exactly the case it exists for.

### Principal types — why this must be consistent across systemuser and CIAM

| Type | Door | Record permission from |
|---|---|---|
| **1** systemuser, licensed | workforce Entra | Dataverse's real answer (impersonated read) ∪ contact grants |
| **2** customer employee, no licence, has contact | workforce Entra (**multitenant**, per-customer consent) | `sprk_assigned*` ∪ org — **no business unit** |
| **3** external contact | **CIAM** | `sprk_assigned*` ∪ org — **no business unit** |

**Term 1 is Type 1 only.** Types 2 and 3 have **no Dataverse answer at all** — a `contact` is not a
security principal: it cannot be a POA share target and cannot be impersonated. That is *why* the contact
plane must **compute** access rather than read it. Any parent-fallback that delegates to "ask Dataverse
about the parent" therefore works for Type 1 and **returns nothing for Types 2 and 3** — their parent
access itself comes from terms 2–4, which only our evaluator computes.

**This is the consistency requirement for your fallback**: a filter-level "evaluate the same right on the
parent" is correct and complete **for Type 1**. For Types 2/3 it is correct but *empty* unless it routes
through the same code that computes terms 2–4. Please scope your fallback explicitly to Type 1 and say so,
rather than leaving it looking universal.

### Core vs child

- **Core** (need direct grants): `sprk_project`, `sprk_matter`, `sprk_workassignment`, `sprk_servicerequest`
- **Child** (inherit **1 hop**): invoice, communication, **document**, event, to-do, analysis
- **Matter does NOT inherit from Project** — both are core, deliberately

**FR-26**: `RegardingResolver` denormalizes the **ultimate core-record ancestor** onto every child,
re-stamped on reparent — so a To Do regarding an Email regarding a Matter carries `sprk_regardingmatter`
directly and access resolves in **one hop**. Chains never need traversal; ADR-034's 1-hop cap holds
unamended.

**FR-27**: children inherit their core ancestor's rights. Acceptance: *a contact with access to Project 1
sees its invoices, events, communications and To Dos; per-child revocation removes one child without
affecting the parent.*

### Two questions your plan raises, answered from spec

- **Which right does the child get?** The **same** right. Term 5 is literally "child takes its core
  ancestor's rights" — no mapping, no reduction. Your "evaluate the *same* right on the parent" matches.
- **Does Secure block inheritance?** **No.** Term 8 suppresses **terms 3 and 4 only**. Explicit grants
  (term 2) and inheritance (term 5) survive. So a principal with legitimate granted access to a secure
  project **does** see its documents. Secure prevents *deriving* access from lookups and org membership; it
  is not a blanket child block.

## 6. 🔴 Three points nothing specifies yet — read before implementing the fallback

Found while answering §5. These are gaps in **our** spec, not criticisms of yours, and we own closing them.

### 6.1 SECURITY-CRITICAL — does term 5 read the ancestor's PRE-veto or POST-veto rights?

The term order puts inheritance at 5 (additive, **before** the max) and vetoes at 6–8 (**after** the max).
So: when the child evaluates term 5, *which* ancestor rights does it read?

- **Post-veto** (ancestor fully evaluated, vetoes applied) → correct. Requires a **two-pass** evaluation.
- **Pre-veto max** → **Secure leaks through children.** A secure project whose derived + org terms were
  suppressed on the parent would still hand full rights to its documents via term 5.

**Not specified anywhere.** Your filter-level fallback has the identical exposure: if it evaluates the
parent's right *without* applying the parent's Secure/Restricted/deny vetoes, a secure project's documents
become reachable by exactly the principals Secure exists to exclude. **Please apply the parent's vetoes
before allowing on the parent's right.** We are filing this as a spec correction.

### 6.2 A document can have TWO core ancestors

FR-26 says "the ultimate core-record **ancestor**" — singular. But you verified `sprk_matter` and
`sprk_project` are both filing parents, and our model says matter does **not** inherit from project (both
are core). So a document filed under both has **two** core ancestors, and neither spec nor design says
whether term 5 takes the max, the first non-null, or a priority order.

`max()` is the answer consistent with "highest wins" — but it is currently an inference, not a rule. Your
fallback covers "matter AND project" per your own §D; make the combination rule explicit.

### 6.3 Orphans are the dominant case and inheritance gives them NOTHING

You verified this and it is the sharpest point in your plan: REST create **always** produces no parent, as
do all five communication-archive paths, Compose non-PDF promote, document-only uploads, and the client's
unassociated mode.

An orphaned document has no core ancestor → **term 5 contributes nothing**. Its only access is term 1
(Type 1's Dataverse answer — and with **74% of documents owned by service principals**, that is `None`) or
term 2 (an explicit grant nobody creates).

**So parent-fallback does not fix orphaned documents.** That is a separate hole, and it is the majority
case. Neither project currently has a task for it. We are filing one; flagging so you do not assume the
fallback closes your P2 completely.
